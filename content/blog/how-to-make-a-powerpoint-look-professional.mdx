"Professional" is not a style. It is the absence of a dozen small signals that tell the audience the deck was assembled in a hurry by someone who would rather be doing anything else. Remove the signals and the same content reads as considered.

Here are the seven that matter, ranked by how much they change per minute spent. Do them by hand if you have the ten minutes; the last section covers having AI do all seven on a deck you already have.

## 1. One typeface, two weights

The fastest tell of an amateur deck is four fonts. Pick one typeface (the one on your website, if it has one), use its bold for titles and its regular for everything else, and stop. If the deck has a system font because someone opened it on a machine without your brand font, fix that too; nothing looks cheaper than Calibri standing in for something else.

## 2. A title that says something on every slide

"Overview" is not a title. "Revenue grew 31% while headcount stayed flat" is a title. When every slide's title states the point, someone flipping through the deck gets the argument without the presenter, and the presenter gets a script for free. This one change does more for a deck than any design decision.

## 3. Fewer words, bigger

Count the words on your densest slide. Over 80 and it is a document, not a slide. Cut it to the three things the slide is for, set them at a size readable from the back of the room, and move the rest to the notes or an appendix. Empty space is not wasted space; it is what makes the remaining words look deliberate.

## 4. Align everything to a grid

Titles that start at slightly different x positions from slide to slide, a logo that jumps two pixels, boxes that almost line up: individually invisible, collectively the reason a deck feels off. Turn on the guides, pick a left margin, and make every slide obey it.

## 5. Real brand colours, used sparingly

Take the exact colour values from your website or brand guide. Use one as the accent and keep everything else neutral. Six colours on one slide is a rainbow, and rainbows are for weather. A [rebrand of an existing deck](/redesign/rebrand-presentation) is mostly this step applied consistently.

## 6. Charts that make one point

A chart with a legend of eight series makes no point. Decide the one thing the chart is for, colour that series with your accent, grey the rest, and put the point in the title. If the chart is a picture pasted from somewhere else, redraw it; pasted charts never match the deck and audiences can tell.

## 7. Consistent image treatment

Either every photo is full-bleed or every photo sits in the same frame. Either every icon is an outline or every icon is filled. Mixing treatments reads as several decks glued together, which is usually how it happened.

## The one-minute version

Title every slide with its point, cut the words, one typeface, one accent colour, align to a grid. The deck will look professional because it will be organised, and organisation is what professional looks like.
