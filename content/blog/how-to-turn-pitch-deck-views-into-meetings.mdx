You sent your pitch deck. Someone opened it. Now what?

Most people send a deck and hope for the best. Some follow up once and give up. A few keep pestering with generic "just checking in" messages that go nowhere.

The problem is that sending a deck is not the end of your pitch. It's the beginning of a conversation. And the gap between "they opened it" and "they want to meet" is where most deals die.

This guide shows you how to close that gap using engagement data, smart timing, and follow-up messages that actually work.

## Why Most Deck Sends Never Turn Into Meetings

When you email a deck as a PDF attachment or static link, you're flying blind. You don't know if they opened it, which slides they looked at, or whether they shared it with anyone else. So you guess when to follow up and what to say.

Even worse, you treat every recipient the same. The person who skimmed three slides gets the same follow-up as the person who spent ten minutes on your pricing page and forwarded the deck to two colleagues.

The result is predictable. You follow up too early or too late. Your message is generic because you have no idea what they care about. They ignore you, and the opportunity goes cold.

## Use Engagement Data to Know When to Follow Up

The first step is knowing what actually happened after you hit send. Did they open the deck? How long did they spend on it? Which slides did they focus on? Did they share it internally?

This is where [viewer analytics](/features/viewer-analytics) becomes essential. When you send a deck as a trackable link instead of a PDF, you can see exactly who viewed it, when, and for how long. You can see which slides they lingered on and whether they forwarded the link to someone else.

This data tells you three critical things:

**When to follow up.** If someone opens your deck within an hour of receiving it and spends eight minutes reading, follow up that same day. If they open it three days later and skim for ninety seconds, wait and try a different angle. If they haven't opened it at all after a week, your subject line or intro email failed.

**What to say.** If they spent most of their time on your case studies page, mention a relevant success story in your follow-up. If they viewed the pricing slide multiple times, acknowledge that they're evaluating cost and offer to walk them through options. If they shared the link with someone else, ask if you should loop in additional stakeholders.

**How warm the lead is.** Someone who views your deck twice, spends a total of fifteen minutes, and forwards it to three people is screaming "I'm interested." Someone who opens it once for two minutes is not. Prioritize accordingly.

## Craft Follow-Ups Based on What They Actually Did

Generic follow-ups get ignored. Specific follow-ups that reference what the recipient actually did get responses.

### If they viewed the deck quickly

They opened it, but they didn't spend real time with it. Maybe they were on mobile, maybe they got interrupted, or maybe your deck didn't grab them.

Your follow-up should acknowledge this and make it easy for them to engage:

*"Saw you took a quick look at the deck. I know it's a lot to absorb in one pass. Would it help if I walked you through the two slides that matter most for [their specific situation]?"*

You're not asking for a full meeting yet. You're offering to solve a problem (too much information) in a low-commitment way.

### If they spent serious time on specific sections

They're interested, and you know what they care about. Use it.

*"I noticed you spent some time on our [case study/pricing/implementation] section. That tells me you're thinking about [specific concern]. Want to jump on a quick call this week to talk through how that would work for you specifically?"*

This works because it's not a guess. You know what they looked at, so your offer is relevant.

### If they shared the deck internally

This is a buying signal. They're socializing your solution with colleagues or a decision-maker. Your job is to make that internal conversation easier.

*"Looks like you shared the deck with a few people on your team. That's great. If it would help, I'm happy to join a quick call with everyone to answer questions and make sure you have what you need to move forward."*

You're offering to support their internal process, not inserting yourself where you're not wanted.

### If they haven't opened it at all

Your email or subject line didn't work. Try a different angle or a different format.

*"Not sure if my last email got buried. I'll keep this short: we help [specific type of company] solve [specific problem]. If that's relevant, here's a 60-second overview: [link]. If not, no worries."*

Shorter, clearer, and less reliant on them opening a whole deck.

## Time Your Follow-Up Correctly

Timing matters as much as message. If you follow up an hour after sending the deck, you look desperate. If you wait two weeks, the moment is gone.

Here's a sensible framework:

- **Same day or next morning** if they opened it quickly and spent real time with it. Strike while the iron is hot.
- **Two to three days** if they opened it but didn't spend much time. Give them space, then nudge.
- **One week** if they haven't opened it at all. At this point, you're re-engaging, not following up.
- **After every new view** if they come back multiple times. Each new view is a signal that they're thinking about it.

If they shared the deck with someone else, follow up within 24 hours. That internal conversation is happening now, and you want to be part of it.

## Make It Easy to Say Yes

Your goal is not to close the deal in a follow-up email. It's to get a meeting. So make that meeting easy to agree to.

Don't say "Let me know if you'd like to chat." That requires them to write back and suggest a time, which adds friction.

Instead, say "I have 20 minutes open Thursday at 2pm or Friday at 10am. Want to grab one of those slots to talk through [specific thing they care about]?" You're giving them a choice, not asking them to do work.

If your calendar tool supports it, include a link to book directly. The fewer steps between your email and a confirmed meeting, the better.

## What to Do After the Meeting

Once you have the meeting, your deck has done its job. But don't abandon the data. After the call, check if they viewed the deck again. If they did, it means they're reviewing your material and probably talking to others internally.

This is your cue to send a follow-up that reinforces the conversation and makes it easy for them to move forward. Reference something specific from the call, attach any additional material you promised, and suggest a clear next step.
