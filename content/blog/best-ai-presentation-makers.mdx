Every AI presentation maker looks great in its own demo. The differences show up later: when the deck has to follow the shape of a lesson or a board meeting, when the figures get questioned, when the file opens on someone else's laptop, or when you only need one deck and every option is a subscription.

We make one of these tools, so weigh our take accordingly. We have tried to be fair: prices and features below were checked against each vendor's own pricing and help pages in October 2026, and we say where a competitor is the better pick.

## The short answer

- **Fastest first draft you will present from the tool itself:** Gamma.
- **Tidy designs that stay on-grid while you edit:** Beautiful.ai.
- **Already living in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace:** Copilot in PowerPoint, or Gemini in Google Slides.
- **Design-heavy work alongside social posts and print:** Canva.
- **A deck you already have, redesigned without losing a slide or a number:** PitchBoost.
- **A file that must hold up as PowerPoint or PDF, a non-English or right-to-left deck, or one deck without a subscription:** PitchBoost.

## What we judged

- **Structure for the purpose.** A lecture, a board update and a sales pitch run differently. Does the tool change the shape, or pour every topic into the same template?
- **Facts.** AI writes confident numbers. Does the tool cite what it adds, or flag what it cannot trace?
- **Working with the deck you have.** Most presentations start from an old file. Can the tool redesign it and keep the content?
- **The file.** Does the PowerPoint or PDF export look like what you saw on screen, and can you still edit it?
- **Language.** Does it work in the language your audience speaks, including right-to-left scripts?
- **Price of one deck.** Plenty of people need one good presentation, not a subscription.

## The tools, one by one

### Gamma

Gamma is the default answer for a reason. Type a prompt and a polished, card-style deck appears in under a minute, and the same canvas also makes documents and simple websites. It imports PowerPoint and PDF files, an area Gamma 5 (October 2026) put real work into. It works from ChatGPT and Claude and has an API.

Where it is weaker: the deck lives most comfortably in Gamma. Its own help pages note that exports follow its Present Mode, that the PowerPoint slide master comes out blank, and that Google Slides can swap fonts. Credits on the free plan come once and do not refill. [PitchBoost vs Gamma](/compare/pitchboost-vs-gamma) goes deeper.

### Beautiful.ai

Beautiful.ai's smart templates rearrange themselves as you add content, so slides stay aligned even when you are in a hurry. It is a good fit for teams that care most about consistency. There is no free plan, and the shortest commitment is a single month at $45.

### Canva

Canva is a design suite first, with AI presentation features alongside social posts, documents and print. If your presentation sits inside a wider design workflow, it is hard to beat. You still do more of the writing and structuring yourself than with a tool built only for decks.

### Copilot in PowerPoint and Gemini in Google Slides

If your organization already pays for Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace with AI included, start here: there is nothing new to buy or learn. Gemini's full-deck builder launched in US English only. Both are strongest on fresh drafts inside their own app. For redesigning an old deck in your brand while keeping every word, they are not the specialist tools.

### Plus AI, Presentations.AI and Pitch

Plus AI works inside PowerPoint and Google Slides, which suits people who never want to leave those apps. Presentations.AI turns business documents into editable decks and has a ChatGPT app. Pitch is a collaborative workspace for teams who build decks together; its free plan does not export to PowerPoint.

### PitchBoost

PitchBoost only makes presentations, and it is built around the moments where the generic tools struggle:

- **Shaped for the purpose.** A lesson ends on takeaways and questions, a report leads with the result and the decision it needs, and a pitch is written for one specific prospect.
- **Your deck, kept.** Upload a PowerPoint or PDF and get one redesigned slide per original slide, with every word and number kept and checked.
- **Facts.** Figures it adds come with a source on the slide. Anything it cannot trace is flagged for you to check, or removed in pitches and reports.
- **Files that hold up.** Download an exact-look PowerPoint or an editable one with real text boxes, plus PDF and Google Slides.
- **Any language.** Right-to-left scripts like Arabic and Hebrew stay right-to-left in the editable PowerPoint too.
- **Price.** Your first deck each month is free as a link. Finishing one deck for download is $9 once; plans start at $7 a month.
- **In ChatGPT.** It runs inside ChatGPT, free accounts included.

Where it is weaker: PitchBoost does not make documents or websites, and its strength is the finished deck rather than a whiteboard for brainstorming.

## Which one for which presentation

- **A class presentation or school project:** PitchBoost or Gamma for a fast start. If the class computer needs a PowerPoint file, PitchBoost. See [class presentations](/presentations/class-presentation).
- **Lecture or lesson slides:** PitchBoost, for the lesson structure and cited facts. See [lecture slides](/presentations/lecture-slides).
- **A thesis defense:** PitchBoost, especially if your results already live in a draft deck. See [thesis defense presentations](/presentations/thesis-defense).
- **A board update or quarterly report:** PitchBoost, or Copilot if your figures already sit in Microsoft 365. See [report presentations](/presentations/report-presentation).
- **A deck for a sales prospect:** PitchBoost, which researches the prospect and writes for them.
- **A social post, poster and slides in one style:** Canva.
- **A quick internal draft you will present from the browser:** Gamma.
- **An old deck that needs to look current:** PitchBoost's [PowerPoint redesign](/redesign/powerpoint-redesign).

## How to choose in ten minutes

Take one real presentation you have to give, not a demo topic. Run it through your two shortlisted tools: once from a prompt, and once from your existing slides if you have them. Then open the exported PowerPoint on another computer. The tool that survives that test is the one to pay for.
