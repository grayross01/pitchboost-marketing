A rebrand lands in the deck library as a chore: a new logo, a new palette, perhaps a new typeface, and every presentation anyone might send now needs all three applied. Done by hand, each deck is an hour of find-and-replace with a mouse. Done badly, the sales team keeps sending the old one because it still works.

Here is what a rebrand of a deck actually involves, and how to get through a library of them in an afternoon.

## What changes in a rebrand (it is not only the logo)

- **The logo**, on the title slide, in the corner of every other slide, and on any closing slide. Watch for the old logo baked into screenshots and photos.
- **The colours**, everywhere the old accent appeared: headings, chart series, table headers, highlight boxes, icons. Chart colours are the ones people forget.
- **The typeface**, if it changed. This one reflows every slide, which is why manual rebrands tend to stop at the colours.
- **The tone**, if the rebrand came with a new voice. A playful new brand on top of formal old copy reads as unfinished.
- **The imagery**, if the brand moved from stock photos to illustration or vice versa.

## The manual route, for one deck

1. Open the slide master, replace the logo and the theme colours, swap the theme fonts. This fixes every slide that actually uses the master.
2. Then fix every slide that does not: manually coloured text, pasted charts, text boxes with hard-coded fonts. In a deck that has been edited by six people, that is most of them.
3. Rebuild charts whose colours were set by hand.
4. Replace images with the old logo in them.
5. Check every slide at full size.

An hour for a clean deck, half a day for a messy one. Fine for one. Not fine for forty.

## The AI route, for the library

A rebuilder reads each slide's content and lays it out again in the new brand, so hard-coded colours and rogue fonts do not survive; they were never copied. That is the difference between restyling (which inherits the mess) and rebuilding (which does not).

## What to do about the appendix problem

Every library has decks with a 20-slide appendix that has not been opened since the deck was made. A rebrand is the moment to ask whether it still earns its place. Rebuild the main deck one-to-one and the appendix in condense mode, or drop it and keep the old file for the archive.
