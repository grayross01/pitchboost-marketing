**Short answer:** yes, with limits. ChatGPT is excellent at the thinking part of a presentation: the outline, the slide copy, the speaker notes and the questions you should prepare for. It can also create a basic .pptx file that opens in PowerPoint. What it does not do well on its own is design: its slides use plain, generic layouts, it does not know your brand, and it cannot tell you whether anyone opened the deck. For a designed deck in your brand, or to redesign a PowerPoint you already have, connect a presentation tool such as PitchBoost inside ChatGPT, or upload the deck to one directly.

Here is what that looks like in practice.

## Can ChatGPT make a PowerPoint (.pptx) file?

Yes. Ask for a presentation and ChatGPT can write the content, then produce a downloadable .pptx, usually by running code behind the scenes. The file opens in PowerPoint, Keynote and Google Slides, and every slide is editable.

What you get is a draft, not a finished deck:

- **Plain layouts.** Titles and bullets on simple backgrounds, similar from one deck to the next.
- **No brand.** It does not know your logo, colors or fonts unless you describe them, and even then it approximates.
- **Text-first slides.** It tends to put everything in bullets. Charts, diagrams and real visual hierarchy are hit and miss.
- **You still do the design pass.** Most people open the file and spend an hour fixing it.

For an internal draft, a class exercise or a first version to react to, that is often enough. For something you will present to a room, a client or an examiner, it usually is not.

## Can ChatGPT redesign a PowerPoint I already have?

This is the question most people are really asking. You have a deck. It is text-heavy, inconsistent or dated, and you want it to look professional without starting over.

On its own, ChatGPT can read an uploaded .pptx and help with the content: suggest a clearer structure, rewrite dense slides, tighten titles, and point out slides that try to do too much. It can also produce a new .pptx with those changes. But it rebuilds the slides as a new, plain file, so the result looks like ChatGPT's default style rather than a designed deck, and images, charts and layouts from the original do not always survive.

A real redesign needs three things a chat alone does not have: a design system built for slides, your brand, and a way to keep every number exactly as you wrote it. That is what a dedicated redesign tool adds.

## How to redesign a PowerPoint without leaving ChatGPT

PitchBoost is available as an app inside ChatGPT. Once it is connected, the whole redesign happens in the chat:

1. **Add PitchBoost** from ChatGPT's app directory and sign in to PitchBoost once (a free account works).
2. **Attach your .pptx** in a chat and ask for what you want: "make this look professional", "redesign this in our brand", or "clean this up for my class presentation".
3. **Point it at a website** for the brand: your company's, your university's, or your own. PitchBoost takes the logo, colors and voice from that site.
4. **Get the result back in the chat.** A few minutes later ChatGPT replies with a link to the finished deck, rebuilt slide by slide in your brand, with an editable PowerPoint and a PDF to download.

Your content and numbers are kept, and you choose whether PitchBoost leaves your wording as it is or condenses dense slides. The [ChatGPT help article](/help/connect-chatgpt) has the setup details. If you would rather not use ChatGPT for this step, [upload the deck to PitchBoost directly](/features/upload-and-redesign); the result is the same.

Google Slides and Keynote decks work too. Export them to .pptx first (File, Download, Microsoft PowerPoint in Google Slides; File, Export To, PowerPoint in Keynote).

## What ChatGPT is genuinely good at for presentations

Use it for the parts it does best, even if the design happens elsewhere:

- **Outlining.** Give it the topic, the audience and the time limit, and ask for a slide-by-slide structure. A well-prompted outline is a far better start than a blank slide.
- **Writing and tightening.** Paste a dense slide and ask for a version under 30 words with a title that states the point.
- **Speaker notes and scripts.** Ask for what to say on each slide, then practice from it.
- **Rehearsal.** Ask it to play a skeptical professor, client or investor and fire questions at you. This is one of the most useful and least used tricks.
- **Research.** Background on a topic, a company or an industry before you build.

## Know whether anyone read it

A .pptx from ChatGPT is a file. Once you email it, it tells you nothing. A PitchBoost deck goes out as a link, so you see when it is opened and when the reader comes back to it. On Pro you also see how long each slide was read. That matters whether the reader is an investor, a client or a professor who asked for the slides before class.
