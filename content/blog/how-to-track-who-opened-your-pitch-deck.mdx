You spent hours crafting the perfect pitch deck. You sent it to a prospect. Then… silence.

Did they even open it? Which slides caught their attention? Did they share it with their team? Without answers to these questions, you're flying blind, guessing at the right time and message for your follow-up.

Tracking who opens your pitch deck and how they engage with it changes everything. You know when someone's interested, which parts resonated, and exactly when to reach out. Here's how to set up deck tracking and use the data to close more deals.

## Why Pitch Deck Tracking Matters

Email open rates tell you almost nothing. Someone might open your email, glance at your message, and move on without ever clicking your attachment or link. Even if they download a PDF, you have no idea whether they looked at it, skimmed two slides, or studied every page.

Deck tracking solves this problem. When you share your presentation as a trackable link instead of an attachment, you can see:

- Who opened your deck and when
- How long they spent viewing it
- Which slides they looked at and which they skipped
- Whether they returned for a second or third look
- If they forwarded it to colleagues (based on new viewer sessions)

This intelligence transforms your follow-up from generic check-ins to informed conversations.

## How to Set Up Pitch Deck Tracking

### Option 1: Use a Presentation Platform with Built-In Analytics

The easiest approach is to use a tool designed for trackable presentations. Instead of sending a PowerPoint or PDF attachment, you upload your deck to a platform that hosts it as a web link and captures viewer data.

When you share that link, the platform records every interaction. No special setup required on your end, and your prospect doesn't need to install anything or create an account. They just click and view.

[Viewer analytics](/features/viewer-analytics) tools typically show you a timeline of views, which slides got the most attention, and whether the viewer engaged deeply or bounced quickly.

### Option 2: PDF Tracking Services

If you must send a PDF, some services let you upload it and generate a trackable link. These work similarly to presentation platforms but with less granular data. You'll usually see who opened the file and roughly how long they spent, but slide-by-slide analytics are often limited or unavailable.

### Option 3: Email Link Tracking (Limited Value)

Basic link tracking through your email platform or a URL shortener tells you if someone clicked, but nothing about what happened after. This is better than nothing, but it won't give you the slide-level insights that make tracking truly useful.

## What to Look for in Your Analytics

Once you're tracking deck views, focus on these key signals:

### Time Spent Viewing

A five-second view means they clicked by accident or immediately decided it wasn't relevant. A five-minute view suggests genuine interest. Most quality presentations take two to four minutes to review properly, so look for viewing times in that range or longer.

### Repeat Views

When someone opens your deck multiple times, especially on different days, they're seriously considering your offer. They might be reviewing details, comparing your solution to alternatives, or preparing to present it internally.

### Slide Engagement Patterns

Which slides did they linger on? If they spent extra time on your pricing page, cost is probably top of mind. If they skipped your company background but studied your case studies, they care about results more than credentials.

Slides that get skipped consistently might be unnecessary or poorly positioned. Slides that always get attention are doing their job.

### Multiple Viewers from the Same Company

If you see three different people from the same organization viewing your deck, it's being shared internally. That's a strong buying signal. It also tells you this isn't a single decision-maker situation, so your follow-up strategy should acknowledge the committee.

## How to Use Tracking Data in Your Follow-Up

### Timing Your Outreach

Don't follow up on a schedule. Follow up based on engagement. If someone views your deck on Tuesday afternoon, reach out Wednesday morning while it's fresh in their mind. If they haven't opened it after three days, your follow-up should focus on re-engaging them, not assuming they've reviewed your materials.

### Personalizing Your Message

Reference what they looked at. "I noticed you spent time on our integration capabilities" is far more compelling than "Just checking in to see if you had questions." You're proving you pay attention and you're ready to discuss what actually interests them.

If they skipped certain sections, ask if those topics aren't relevant or if they'd like more detail. If they returned to specific slides multiple times, offer to dive deeper on those points.

### Identifying Hot Leads

Prioritize prospects who:
- View your deck within hours of receiving it
- Spend significant time reviewing it thoroughly
- Come back for multiple views
- Generate additional viewers from their organization

These behaviors indicate serious interest. They deserve faster, more attentive follow-up than prospects who glance briefly and never return.

### Adjusting Your Deck

Tracking data across multiple prospects reveals patterns. If everyone skips slides 6 through 9, cut them. If everyone lingers on slide 12, consider moving similar content earlier. If viewing times are consistently short, your deck might be too long or failing to hook attention early.

This feedback loop lets you continuously improve your materials based on real behavior, not guesswork.

## Common Tracking Mistakes to Avoid

**Don't stalk your prospects.** Just because you can see someone opened your deck at 11 p.m. doesn't mean you should reference that in your follow-up. Use the data to inform your strategy, not to creep people out.

**Don't over-interpret single data points.** One person skipping your case studies doesn't mean case studies are useless. Look for patterns across multiple viewers before making changes.

**Don't rely only on analytics.** Tracking data enhances your follow-up but doesn't replace actual conversations. Use insights to start better discussions, not to avoid picking up the phone.

**Don't send attachments after setting up tracking.** If you've invested in trackable links, actually use them. Falling back to PDF attachments defeats the purpose and leaves you blind again.

## Making Tracking Part of Your Process

The real power of deck tracking comes from making it routine, not occasional. Every pitch deck you send should be trackable. Every follow-up should reference engagement data. Every month, review your aggregate analytics to spot trends and opportunities for improvement.

This discipline separates sales teams that guess from teams that know. You'll close more deals because you're reaching out at the right moments with the right messages, and you'll waste less time on prospects who were never serious to begin with.
